    Semantic Findings

    Every figure below was computed from the committed Lab 8 data with lab8/analyze_lab8.py and re-checked by lab8/validate_lab8.py. These are descriptive results, not causal claims.

    1. What are the major semantic topics?

    KMeans on the 384-dimensional embeddings yields 10 interpretable topics across the 1,448 passages. The largest are Culture, Literature & the Arts (205 passages, 14.2%), Politics, Policy & Economics (201, 13.9%), Mathematics, Physical Science & Computing (187, 12.9%) and Grading, Registration & Academic Standing (149, 10.3%); the smallest is Biology, Environment & Global Health (88, 6.1%). The topics split cleanly into administrative themes (grading/registration, transfer and placement credit, advising) and disciplinary themes that mirror the course catalogue, which is expected because Part 10 supplies most of the corpus.

    2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

    Administrative topics are the most spread out. Student Development, Advising & Skills appears in 58 of the 151 formal sections, and Transfer Credit & Global Education and Placement Credit & Degree Requirements each appear in 54 — these concepts recur throughout the bulletin rather than living in one chapter. Disciplinary topics are far more concentrated: Biology, Environment & Global Health touches only 18 sections and Language & Writing Courses only 24. This is the core contrast between formal and semantic structure: some meanings are localized to their section, others are woven across the whole document.

    3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

    Diversity is measured as the Shannon entropy of a section's topic distribution (sections with at least 15 passages). The most diverse are the interdisciplinary majors: Computation and Design (27 passages spanning 7 topics, entropy 2.610) and Degree Requirements (40 passages, 5 topics, entropy 2.058). Size alone does not create diversity: Mathematics (MATH) has 28 passages — about the same as Computation and Design — yet sits in just 2 topics (entropy 0.222), and Chinese (CHINESE), despite 36 passages, sits entirely in one topic (entropy 0.000) — the least diverse section by this measure. Broad, cross-cutting majors mix many meanings; single-discipline course lists do not.

    4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

    Computed in the original embedding space, the highest cross-section similarities are near-identical cross-listed course descriptions that appear under two formal sections. Passage p0744 in Biology (BIOL) and p0928 in Environment (ENVIR) are the same "Ecosystem services…" description (cosine 1.000), and p0552 in Global China Studies matches p0677 in Political Economy on "POLECON 401 / GCHINA 401 Political Economy of the Chinese Reform" (cosine 1.000). The two rows in each pair differ only in trivial spacing or capitalization, so they survive exact-duplicate removal yet remain semantically equivalent — cross-listed courses act as semantic bridges linking otherwise separate majors, exactly the kind of connection the nearest-neighbour interaction surfaces.

    5. Search concept: does "credit" occupy one topic or several?

    The word "credit" appears in 181 passages spread across 9 of the 10 topics, so it is a genuinely cross-cutting concept rather than a single semantic region. It concentrates in Placement Credit & Degree Requirements (81 passages) and Transfer Credit & Global Education (46) — together 70% — but still surfaces in Grading, Registration & Academic Standing (27) and Language & Writing Courses (10). By contrast "graduation" (33 passages) is confined to just 4 topics, mostly Transfer Credit (12) and Grading/Registration (11). Lexical search and semantic neighbourhood therefore answer different questions.

    Design Description

    Method, encodings, and rationale (approximately 250 words).

    Embedding model
    sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2
    Embedding dimension
    384 (L2-normalized)
    Projection
    UMAP · n_neighbors 15 · min_dist 0.15 · metric cosine · seed 401
    Clustering
    KMeans on original embeddings · k = 10 · n_init 20 · seed 401

    Each passage is embedded with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence-transformer, producing L2-normalized 384-dimensional vectors so that cosine similarity measures meaning. Two reductions run on these vectors. UMAP (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0.15, cosine metric, seed 401) projects them to two dimensions purely for layout, while KMeans (k = 10, n_init 20, seed 401) clusters the full-dimensional vectors — never the UMAP coordinates — so topics reflect true semantic structure. The number of clusters was chosen from a k = 8–12 silhouette and interpretability scan; k = 10 gave balanced, coherent groups. Each cluster was labelled by hand after reading its top TF-IDF terms and representative passages, and those labels are frozen in source for reproducibility.

    The semantic map encodes UMAP position, topic colour (a ten-hue categorical palette), and word count as a square-root-scaled radius, so area, not radius, tracks length. Nearest neighbours are computed in the original embedding space and marked with a warm ring, and the selected passage with a dark ring, so highlighting never relies on colour alone. The Topic×Section matrix summarizes what the map cannot: how each topic distributes across 151 formal sections. A single central state function drives search, topic and section filters (combined with AND), the matrix selection, the selected point and its neighbours, keeping the two views coordinated and their emphasis deterministic rather than accumulated across handlers.

    Important limitations

    • The map is a 2D UMAP approximation; distance on the map is less faithful than similarity in the original embedding space.
    • Topic labels are interpretive human summaries of statistical clusters, not official bulletin categories.
    • PDF extraction and TOC-based hierarchy parsing may contain imperfections (for example cross-listed courses appearing under multiple sections).
    • Free-text search is lexical (substring match), whereas the neighbour highlighting is embedding-based; they can disagree.
    • Part 10 (Majors and Courses) supplies most passages, so disciplinary topics dominate the corpus by construction.
