The dataset and how to read the network

Twelve fabricated companies trade with each other over 60 days, from 1 January to 1 March 2026. Every company is a node and stays in the same place for the whole animation. A line is drawn only for the commercial relationships recorded on the day being shown, so lines appear and disappear as time advances while the twelve companies never move. Relationships are treated as undirected. Data: lab7_assignment_companies.csv and lab7_assignment_transactions_60days.csv, loaded in the browser with d3.csv().

Loading the commercial network…

Mental map. A single d3.forceSimulation() is run once over the twelve companies and the 27 relationships that occur at any point in the 60 days. The layout is ticked to a stable state before the first frame and the resulting coordinates are then held fixed, with a regional pull that keeps each company inside its labelled band, so any line crossing a band boundary is a cross-region relationship. The relationship and repulsion forces set the order within a band. The simulation is never restarted for a new day, so nothing on screen changes except the things that genuinely change in the data: which lines exist, how thick they are, how large each company is, and the summary values.

Temporal Network Questions

All figures below were computed from the two supplied CSV files with lab7/analyze_lab7.py and re-checked by lab7/validate_lab7.py. The 60 days are split into three 20-day windows. These are descriptive counts of commercial activity, not causal claims.

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

Connectivity is almost flat for the first 40 days and then rises. Days 1–20 average 5.50 active relationships per day and days 21–40 average 5.60, but days 41–60 average 7.25. Participation moves the same way: 8.55, then 8.05, then 9.65 of the twelve companies are active on an average day. Daily transaction value grows faster than the link count, from about $77,196 per day in the first window to $96,770 and then $144,326, because the average relationship also gets larger ($14,036, $17,280, $19,907). The sparsest frames are all early — days 1, 7, 11, 13, 17 and 19 each carry only 3 relationships — while days 45, 51 and 57 are the densest, with 10 relationships and all twelve companies active.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

The lead changes twice. In days 1–20 activity is spread fairly evenly and Fusion Electronics (c06) is only a modest leader, with $372,117 of incident volume over 27 incident daily-link records. In days 21–40 Fusion Electronics becomes the clear hub: $1,053,610 and 53 incident records, more than twice the next company, Granite Wholesale (c07) at $484,156. In days 41–60 it hands over to Ion Systems (c09) at $1,096,550 across 46 records and Cedar Retail (c03) at $901,810 across 43 records, while Fusion Electronics falls back to $559,804 and keeps only three trading partners. Ion Systems grows from two partners in each of the first two windows to five in the last, and Cedar Retail from three and two to five.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

The daily network is fragmented throughout — counting connected components among the active companies only, no single day of the 60 forms one connected component. Three components is the usual state (34 days), two components happens on 20 days and four on days 3, 4, 9, 16, 28 and 40. Day 1 is the clearest example: 3 relationships, 6 active companies and 3 separate pairs. Fragmentation is not simply an early-period problem either. The three peak days, 45, 51 and 57, have all twelve companies active and 10 relationships, yet they still split into 3 components. The late period adds more relationships around the same active companies rather than stitching the separate groups together.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

Twenty-seven distinct relationships occur across the 60 days, and only 9 of them appear in all three windows. The most persistent strong one is Delta Components (c04) – Fusion Electronics (c06): active on 20 separate days spread across the whole period, worth $440,146 in total. Three relationships stop after day 20, including Cedar Retail (c03) – Fusion Electronics (c06), which was active on 10 days in the first window and never returns; Fusion Electronics (c06) – Granite Wholesale (c07) and Granite Wholesale (c07) – Harbor Shipping (c08) run through day 40 and then stop. Eight relationships are new in days 41–60, and the largest of them arrive with real weight straight away: Fusion Electronics (c06) – Ion Systems (c09), first seen on day 42, is active on 10 days for $300,580; Cedar Retail (c03) – Keystone Materials (c11), first seen on day 41, reaches $282,296 over 10 days; Cedar Retail (c03) – Granite Wholesale (c07), also from day 41, reaches $254,352.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

More cross-regional, and the change is concentrated in the final window. Cross-region pairs are 63.64% of the daily relationship records in days 1–20 and 63.39% in days 21–40 — essentially unchanged — then 72.41% in days 41–60. The mechanism is visible in the new relationships: 7 of the 8 relationships that first appear in the last window link companies in different regions, including Cedar Retail (North America) with Keystone Materials (Asia), Granite Wholesale (Europe) and Harbor Shipping (Europe), and Ion Systems (North America) with Fusion Electronics (Asia), Apex Textiles (Asia) and Delta Components (Europe). On the network these read as dashed lines crossing between the regional bands, and they are the lines that arrive late and stay.
